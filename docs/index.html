Introduction

Hybrid language models replace most softmax-attention layers with linear-attention ones and keep only a few full-attention layers, which carry the model’s global recall. We find that chain-of-thought supervised fine-tuning damages exactly those layers: reasoning accuracy goes up, but long-range retrieval collapses — HypeNet-9B drops from 67.2% to 9.4% on NIAH-Single-2 at 256K context after CoT-SFT.

We trace the failure to gradient locality: under a stationary, reversible ergodic CoT-Markov model, the average magnitude of the loss gradient on attention logits decays exponentially with the distance between tokens, so the fine-tuning signal rewrites short-range routing and leaves long-range routing unsupervised.

This suggests a remedy that needs no training at all. QK-Restore copies WQ and WK from the pre-SFT checkpoint back into every retained softmax-attention layer, and keeps WV, WO, the linear-attention layers and the FFNs at their post-SFT values — routing is restored while the adaptation that produced the reasoning gains is preserved. Across Jet-Nemotron-2B and HypeNet-2B/5B/9B, QK-Restore recovers up to +23.2 points of NIAH-Single-3 recall at 128K and +19.8 points at 256K over CoT-SFT. Most of the reasoning gain survives: Avg@16 on MATH500 and GSM8K stays within 0.7 points of CoT-SFT on three of the four models (HypeNet-9B gives up 2.2 and 3.0 points), and Maj@16 often improves. It requires zero training, no data and no optimization — only the matched pre-SFT checkpoint.

QK-Restore

Q and K decide where attention goes; V and O decide what it does with the tokens it attends to. CoT-SFT drifts the routing, so QK-Restore puts the routing back and leaves everything else alone:

  1. Start from the post-SFT checkpoint.
  2. Copy WQ and WK from the pre-SFT checkpoint into every softmax-attention layer.
  3. Keep WV, WO, linear-attention and FFN weights unchanged.

The whole method is a weight transplant — no extra data, no optimization steps, no hyperparameters beyond the choice of layers (we restore all retained softmax layers and heads with the same rule).

Reasoning Improves, Recall Falls

The loss grows with task difficulty and with context length: on HypeNet-2B, CoT-SFT costs about 40 points of NIAH-Single-2 accuracy and 23 points of NIAH-Single-3 accuracy at long contexts. The models still read; they stop retrieving.

Why: Local Gradients, Distant Memories

Let g(τ) be the average absolute loss gradient on attention logits, over positions, retained softmax layers and heads, for token pairs τ apart. Under the paper’s assumptions it obeys an exponentially decaying bound:

g(τ) ≤ C′e−τ/W = C′ρτ,   0 < ρ < 1

Both the token autocorrelation of the training data and the measured attention-gradient magnitudes are well fitted by exponential decay. The gradient window is short in absolute terms — W ≈ 41 tokens for the CoT post-SFT model and ≈ 48 tokens for the pre-SFT model — so the fine-tuning signal effectively supervises short-range routing only, and long-range routing is left to drift.

The Objective Is the Cause

Swapping the CoT data for Tulu-3 instruction tuning, with the same models and the same contexts, recall does not collapse — it improves. The damage comes from the chain-of-thought objective, not from fine-tuning as such. QK-Restore tracks the fine-tuned model closely here: ahead at the longest context (+1.4 points of NIAH-Single-3 at 256K on both models), within about two points elsewhere, and never worse on instruction following (IFEval 26.6 vs. 26.6 and 29.0 vs. 27.2).
